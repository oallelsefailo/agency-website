Adobe Commerce Intelligence Pricing: What It Costs and When It Makes Sense

Adobe does not publish a price for Commerce Intelligence. You talk to sales and you get a quote. Here is what shapes that quote, what the tool actually does, and an honest way to decide whether you need it at all.

Where the product came from

Adobe Commerce Intelligence started life as RJMetrics, a standalone analytics company. Magento bought it in 2016 and renamed it Magento Business Intelligence. When Adobe bought Magento in 2018, it got renamed again. Three names, same core product: a cloud data warehouse with dashboards built for commerce data.

That history matters for one reason. A lot of what you read about "Magento BI pricing" is from the old era, when there was a public price list. That list is gone.

How the pricing actually works now

There is no public price. Commerce Intelligence is licensed as an add-on to Adobe Commerce, and the number comes out of a sales conversation. What moves it: how much data you have, how many people need seats, and what else is in your Adobe contract.

For rough calibration, the old Magento BI list had a starter tier around a hundred dollars a month and a Pro tier that ran well into four figures. Merchants who share their quotes today generally talk in four figures a month once the store has real order volume. Your quote may differ, which is sort of the point of quote-based pricing.

If budget matters to you, ask for the number in writing early in the conversation, before the demo cycle eats a month.

What you get for the money

To be fair to the product, it is a real BI tool. You get an actual data warehouse, a SQL report builder, cohort analysis, customer lifetime value work, and scheduled email reports. If you pipe other data sources in, you can combine them. For a team with an analyst who lives in this stuff, it is genuinely capable.

When it earns the price

The honest cases: you run an enterprise Adobe Commerce store, you have an analyst or a data team in-house, and you need deep cohort, margin, or lifetime value analysis on a schedule. Board reporting, multi-brand rollups, custom SQL against warehoused history. If that is your world, a real BI tool is the right purchase and this one is built for your platform.

When it is overkill

Most stores that look up this pricing are not that. They are mid-market stores where the owner or a marketing lead does the reporting, nobody writes SQL, and the actual weekly questions are simple: what changed, which products moved, where did traffic come from, what should I fix first.

For that job, a warehouse and a report builder is a lot of tool. The dashboards still need a person to read them, the SQL builder still needs someone who writes SQL, and the quote still lands every month whether anyone logged in or not.

Alternatives worth knowing about

Build it yourself. GA4 plus Looker Studio costs nothing in licenses. You own the plumbing, the connectors, and the maintenance, and getting order-line detail out of your Magento database into it is its own project. The licenses cost nothing. Your time is another story.

General BI tools. Power BI or Metabase pointed at a read replica of your database. Cheaper licenses than Adobe, very flexible, and still needs someone to build the dashboards and keep them honest.

The short version

If you have the analyst and the enterprise budget, Adobe Commerce Intelligence is a legitimate tool and you should get the quote and evaluate it properly. If what you actually want is to open one page on Monday and know how the store is doing and what needs attention, you can get that without a sales cycle, for a price that is on a public pricing page.
